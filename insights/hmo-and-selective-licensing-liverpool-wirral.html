Licensing shared homes in Liverpool and Wirral: what applies in 2026

Parklane Insights · Property · October 2026

Anyone who owns or manages rented homes in Merseyside now has two licensing questions to answer for every property: does it need an HMO licence, and is it in a selective licensing area? The answers decide what paperwork, fees and standards apply before a single room is let. This article sets out the rules as published by government and the two councils.

Mandatory HMO licensing: the national rule

Across England, a house in multiple occupation (HMO) needs a mandatory licence from the local council if it is occupied by five or more people forming two or more separate households. Since 1 October 2018 this applies whatever the number of storeys. The test is set out in the Licensing of Houses in Multiple Occupation (Prescribed Description) (England) Order 2018.

A licensed HMO must also meet minimum bedroom sizes, which are mandatory licence conditions under the 2018 Mandatory Conditions Regulations:

  • 6.51 m² for a room used by one person aged over 10;
  • 10.22 m² for a room used by two people aged over 10;
  • 4.64 m² for a room used by one child under 10. A room smaller than this cannot be used as sleeping accommodation at all.

Any part of the floor where the ceiling height is below 1.5 metres does not count towards the room's floor area. Councils can also set their own standards for kitchens, bathrooms and amenities, so the national minimum is a starting point rather than the whole picture.

Smaller shared houses, with three or four people from two or more households, are still HMOs under the Housing Act 2004. They may not need a mandatory licence, but the HMO management regulations still apply, and so may any local additional or selective scheme.

Liverpool: selective licensing

Liverpool City Council's selective licensing scheme started on 1 April 2022 and covers designated wards. In those areas, anyone renting a property to tenants must hold a licence, unless the property has an HMO licence or another exemption applies. The council publishes an address checker and a public register of licensed properties.

The council's published fee for a full property licence is £704, paid in two parts: £232.32 on application and £471.68 before the licence is granted. Lower fees apply in some cases: - properties new to the rental market that apply before letting or within 14 days; - a £64 reduction for an EPC rating of C or above; - a £64 reduction where all flats in a block are licensed by the same holder; - a £37 reduction for members of named professional bodies, including Propertymark, safeagent, the NRLA and the North West Property Owners Association. Membership must already be in place when you apply.

Fees are not refunded if a licence is refused or revoked.

Under section 84 of the Housing Act 2004, a selective licensing designation can last no more than five years. Owners in the designated wards should therefore watch for the council's decision on what follows the current scheme.

Wirral: a new scheme from October 2025

Wirral Council designated six areas for selective licensing from 1 October 2025 to 30 September 2030. They are defined by Lower Super Output Area: - Birkenhead Central; - Lower Tranmere; - Tranmere North; - Hamilton Square B; - Tranmere Lairds; - Egremont South in Wallasey.

The council's public notice lists the streets in each area. Its fee schedule for the scheme sets a standard fee of £605, with discounts for accredited landlords, early applications, multiple properties and properties rated EPC C or above. Applying in instalments or on paper carries extra charges.

The Wirral notice is clear about the consequences of letting a licensable property without a licence: prosecution with an unlimited fine, or a civil penalty of up to £30,000, plus possible rent repayment orders.

What this means in practice

For anyone acquiring or managing shared housing in the region, licensing is a due-diligence item, not an afterthought:

  1. Check the address against the council's selective licensing map before exchange, not after completion.
  2. Count the occupiers and households to see whether a mandatory HMO licence is needed, and check that each bedroom meets the minimum sizes.
  3. Check whether a licence can transfer. Licences are personal to the holder, so a buyer usually needs to apply in their own name. Timing matters for the early-application discounts in both Liverpool and Wirral.
  4. Budget for the fee and for the work the licence conditions may require, such as fire doors, alarms and amenity upgrades.
  5. Diarise renewals. Selective schemes are time-limited and their boundaries can change.

Licensing rules are local and change over time. Always confirm the current position with the council before relying on it.
