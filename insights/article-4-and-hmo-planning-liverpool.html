Article 4 and HMOs in Liverpool: why planning now matters as much as licensing

Parklane Insights · Planning · October 2026

Licensing decides how a shared house must be run. Planning decides whether it can be a shared house at all. In Liverpool the planning question is changing quickly, so anyone considering a conversion or acquisition in the city needs to understand Article 4 directions and where they apply.

The planning classes

Planning law treats an ordinary family home and a small shared house as different uses:

  • Class C3 is a dwellinghouse used by a single household.
  • Class C4 is a house in multiple occupation for three to six unrelated occupants who share basic amenities.
  • Larger HMOs, for seven or more people, fall outside the use classes ("sui generis") and always need planning permission. Liverpool City Council confirms that this applies to all such HMOs anywhere in the city.

Nationally, permitted development rights normally allow a change from C3 to C4 without a planning application.

What an Article 4 direction does

A council can remove that permitted development right for a defined area by making an Article 4 direction under the Town and Country Planning (General Permitted Development) (England) Order 2015. Inside the area, converting a family house into a small HMO then needs full planning permission. The council can weigh issues such as the concentration of existing HMOs, the loss of family housing, parking, waste and local character.

A direction does not ban HMOs. It means each new one is assessed on its merits. HMOs already lawfully in use when a direction takes effect are not affected. Owners who need proof can apply for a lawful development certificate.

Liverpool's existing directions

Liverpool already has Article 4 controls in place. The council's planning page explains that, inside the Article 4 area, converting a property into an HMO for three or more people needs planning permission. Properties already in HMO use for three or more people before 17 June 2021 are not affected. The council publishes the "11 Wards Article 4 Direction" and a separate direction for The Dales, with a map of the area.

The October 2026 proposals

On 13 October 2026, Liverpool's Cabinet considered a report recommending a significant extension:

  • Immediate Article 4 directions for five further areas:
  • Area 1: Kirkdale West and East, Everton North, County, Walton, Orrell Park and part of Fazakerley North and West;
  • Area 2: part of Tuebrook Larkhill;
  • Area 3: Old Swan East and West and part of Stoneycroft;
  • Area 4: Penny Lane and part of Calderstones;
  • Area 5: St Michaels, Sefton Park and part of Festival Gardens.
  • A non-immediate direction covering the rest of the city, which would take effect after a notice period.

Local press reported that Cabinet approved the directions. However, the opposition group then called in the decision on about 20 October 2026, asking for the whole city to be covered immediately. The council's own spokesperson said the call-in would delay directions that had been due to take effect on 20 October. At the time of writing, the outcome of the call-in is still pending.

What this means for owners and buyers

  1. Check the property's lawful use, not just its current use. A house occupied as an HMO without the right planning status can face enforcement. The safest evidence is a lawful development certificate or a planning history showing C4 or sui generis use.
  2. Treat timing as critical. If a non-immediate city-wide direction is made, there will be a defined date after which new C3-to-C4 conversions need permission. A conversion must be genuinely in use before that date to benefit from the permitted development right.
  3. Don't confuse licensing with planning. An HMO licence does not grant planning permission, and planning permission does not remove the need for a licence. A property in Kirkdale or Walton, for example, may need both selective or HMO licensing and, under the proposals, planning permission for a new conversion.
  4. Factor planning risk into valuations. In Article 4 areas, an existing lawful HMO and a family house can be valued quite differently. The difference depends on the evidence of lawful use.
  5. Follow the council's formal notices. Article 4 directions take effect only through published legal notices. Press reports and Cabinet papers show direction of travel, but the notices themselves are what count.

Beyond Liverpool

Other councils in the region use their own mix of licensing and planning controls. For example, Wirral has a selective licensing scheme in parts of Birkenhead, Tranmere and Wallasey from October 2025. Check each council's planning policy and Article 4 register for the specific address.
