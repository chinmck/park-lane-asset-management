The Renters' Rights Act: what changed on 1 May 2026 for shared-house landlords

Parklane Insights · Lettings · October 2026

The Renters' Rights Act 2025 is the biggest change to private renting in England for a generation. Its main tenancy reforms came into effect on 1 May 2026. For owners and managers of room-let and HMO properties, where tenancies start and end more often than in single-family lets, the practical effects are significant. This summary draws on the government's published guidance for landlords.

Every tenancy is now periodic

All existing assured shorthold tenancies have become assured periodic tenancies, and every new tenancy is one too. They run on a rolling basis, for example monthly, and a tenancy agreement can no longer have an end date.

Landlords with a written tenancy agreement did not need to re-sign it. Instead, they had to give their tenants the government's Renters' Rights Act Information Sheet by 31 May 2026. Where a tenancy was purely verbal, the landlord had to give the tenant written information about its key terms by the same date.

Section 21 has gone

Landlords can no longer end a tenancy using the section 21 "no-fault" procedure. To recover possession, a landlord must serve a section 8 notice relying on one or more statutory grounds for possession, and give the correct notice period. The government says this is often four months, although some grounds have shorter periods. If the tenant does not leave, the landlord must apply to court for a possession order.

Several points matter particularly for shared housing:

  • Selling or moving in cannot be used as a ground within the first 12 months of a tenancy.
  • Rent arrears: the mandatory ground requires the tenant to owe three months' rent. Below that, the court may decide the tenant can stay.
  • Antisocial behaviour: notice periods are shorter, and proceedings can begin immediately. This is relevant where one occupier's conduct affects others in a shared house.
  • Students: there is a new ground to relet to students for the next academic year. It covers full-time students on a joint contract in an HMO, and notice must be given at the start of the tenancy. It does not cover all student property.
  • Deposits: where a deposit was taken, a court will only make a possession order if the deposit was properly protected and the prescribed information given, or the deposit has been returned, or the issue has been settled in court.

Letting and rent rules

The guidance also sets out new rules on how homes are let:

  • Landlords cannot ask for, encourage or accept rent before the tenancy agreement is signed.
  • Landlords must not discriminate against applicants who receive benefits or have children.
  • Tenants can ask to keep a pet, and a landlord may refuse only for a valid reason.
  • Adverts must show an asking rent. Landlords must not invite or accept bids above it.
  • Rent can be increased only once a year, using the section 13 procedure with Form 4A and at least two months' notice. Tenants can challenge an increase above the open-market rent at the tribunal.

Stronger enforcement

Local councils have new powers to investigate and act. The list of offences for which a tenant can claim a rent repayment order has been expanded, and the maximum award has risen from one year's rent to two years' rent. Councils can also seek repayment of rent paid through Universal Credit.

What comes next

The Act also provides for a Private Rented Sector Database, a landlord ombudsman and a Decent Homes Standard for private renting. The government has published an implementation roadmap, but these depend on further regulations, so owners should keep an eye on official announcements rather than assume dates.

Practical checklist for room-let owners

  1. Make sure every current tenant received the Information Sheet or written terms by 31 May 2026, and keep proof that it was served.
  2. Retire fixed-term templates and check that agreements and adverts comply with the new rules on rent in advance, bidding and pets.
  3. Check that every deposit is protected and the prescribed information has been served. This is now a precondition of possession.
  4. Keep good records of rent, arrears and any antisocial-behaviour incidents. Possession now depends on evidence for a specific ground.
  5. Use Form 4A for any rent increase, no more than once a year.
